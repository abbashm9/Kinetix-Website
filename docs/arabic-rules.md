# Arabic copy rules — Kinetix website

Audience: business owners across the GCC, Saudi Arabia first. Register: **white Gulf Arabic**, close to MSA but conversational: short sentences, plain words, readable in Riyadh, Jeddah, Kuwait and Dubai alike. Not heavy Kuwaiti dialect, not formal news Arabic.

Source of these rules: `AIOS/references/kuwaiti-dialect-guide.md` (Abbas's corrections log), plus the KSA decision of 2026-10-29.

## Never

- Egyptian or Levantine forms: «ده» → «هذا/هذه», «مين» → «من», bare «حد» → «أحد», «كذا» (meaning "like this") → «هكذا» or rephrase.
- «حچى» in any form. Use «نتكلم / تكلمنا».
- ـون on an imperative. Plural imperatives end in ـوا: «تواصلوا»، «ابدأوا»، «اطلبوا». Never «ابدأون».
- Stiff MSA a Gulf owner wouldn't say out loud («تطيع», «يتوجب عليكم»). If it sounds like a government form, rewrite it.
- Em dashes. Use a comma, a full stop or a colon.
- Translating the English line word for word. Write the Arabic line for the idea, then check it says the same thing.

## Always

- One addressee per page: the business owner, singular. **Masculine singular is the default** for an unspecified owner («تواصل معنا»، «مشروعك»). Check every verb and pronoun against it.
- Numbers in Western digits (420, 2026) to match the English pages and pricing.
- Brand and product names stay in Latin script: Kinetix, Meta, Google, WhatsApp, Instagram.
- `dir="rtl"` and `lang="ar"` on the page; mirror layout, not icons that mean direction-neutral things.
- Put every Arabic string you write into the PR description in one list so Abbas can review the copy in one pass on the screenshots.

## Open

- Saudi-only words («جوال», «أبغى») are fine for Saudi readers but read Saudi to Kuwaitis. Prefer the neutral choice («الهاتف», «أريد / تحتاج») unless Abbas says otherwise.
